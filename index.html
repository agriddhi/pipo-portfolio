01 character sheet

So — what exactly is Pipo?

  1. 01

    Name

    Pipo

    Two syllables, one breath. She answers to it from very far away.

  2. 02

    What she is

    Species: Sakura Dragon. A round, chubby body. Huge sparkly eyes with a whole sky inside them. A long curled tail. Soft pastel scales that shift colour with the time of day — peach at dawn, lavender at dusk, moon-white at midnight. Cream horns like candle flame.

    Personality: curious, fearless, always roaming. Cannot walk past somebody who looks sad.
    Quirks: she sneezes and the sky erupts in cherry blossom petals. She shapeshifts so she can travel light. And she has a genuinely awful sense of humour — she will ragebait her friends and laugh about it for an hour.

  3. 03

    What she does

    Night Shift, Sky Tech Department. Half the job is her star terminal — a private patch of sky where every star carries a line. Some of them are her. Some of them are yours.

    The other half is building things: a flower yard, a mood radio, a machine that cracks fortune cookies. Mission: spread love, joy and positivity. She is not subtle about it.

  4. 04

    Her story

    She was made out of the love that was already floating around the universe — nothing was taken, something was recognised. The Dragon of Moons and Stars raised her up on the moon, where she learned that light is a thing you hand to people.

    When she grew up she came all the way down to look, and found a mountain covered in cherry blossom. She stayed. The stars came with her.

  5. 05

    Tagline

    “Everything is possible with the power of love and gratitude.”

    She says it at least four times a day. She means it every time.

07 how she got here

The long way down.

The sky will change as you scroll. It has opinions about your reading speed.

before · the bit that was already there

Something was loving the universe before anybody noticed

No moon yet. No dragon. Not one cherry blossom. Just a warmth, sitting quietly in the middle of everything, doing nothing in particular.

It wasn’t clever and it wasn’t planning anything. It just kept noticing that every single thing it touched was worth keeping. Then it looked around the room and went: oh. her.

then · the moon

The Dragon of Moons and Stars

She was raised on the moon by a dragon so old that nobody remembers when the counting started. The job was to hold a light without crushing it, which sounds easy right up until you try it four hundred times in a row.

The rule was simple and she never once wrote it down: you do not keep the light. You hand it to somebody, and then you let them carry it.

She was small for ages. She had opinions about that.

after · the long way down

Nobody sent her. She just let go.

That’s the part her guardian wasn’t ready for. No permission, no plan, no note. One morning she simply opened her claws and fell, on purpose, because she wanted to see what the bottom looked like.

Falling was only scary for about eleven seconds. Then the air went pink, and she stopped worrying and started committing to it.

then · the mountain

She landed in a tree. That’s how it started.

It was old and pink and completely unbothered by a small dragon arriving out of the sky at roughly the speed of a dropped piano.

She stayed one night. Then a week. Then she moved into the branches and has defended that decision every single day since, usually at volume.

now · every night since

The stars came with her and never left

They turned up the same night she did — a handful, stuck to her back like they’d travelled a very long way to find somebody specific.

She never asked. She just started handing them out. One line each, to whoever looks up. It’s not a big job. It’s definitely not a small one either.

08 tier 2 · interactive storybook

Pipo's Childhood

Four true things, more or less. Drag, swipe, or use the arrow keys.

i. the petal that would not land

Every spring the tree let go of one extra petal, and every spring that petal refused to fall. This one hung in the air above the ninth roof for three whole days, turning slowly, humming a note only dogs could hear.

On the fourth day it cracked. Something small fell out, looked around, and said “well, this is mine then.”

ii. the worst student on the moon

They taught her to hold a light without crushing it. She crushed every single one. Moon-moths, candle-flames, one beetle. All of it, in one afternoon, with great confidence.

She got it eventually, on a Tuesday. Then she cried about it for six hours, which the guardian counted as the actual lesson.

iii. the fall, and the bucket

She left the moon to look, purely to look. She landed in a bucket. The bucket tipped. She landed in a hedge, which was a considerable improvement and which she has defended in every argument since.

That is the whole secret of flying: not trying, and refusing to stay down.

iv. the tree, and the crooked chimney

She landed in a cherry tree and stayed eleven minutes. Then a week. Then she moved into the branches, permanently, and nobody was ever going to be talked out of it.

Her very first delivery went to the crooked chimney, because it was the only warm one on the mountain. She has been late since. She has never missed.
